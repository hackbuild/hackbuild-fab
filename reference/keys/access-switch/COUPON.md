# Coupon checklist: print and read the access switch spring coupon

The owner's steps for the thickness ladder coupon of the access switch
(`coupon/`, R1-flagship-access-switch-10; plan v9 section 6 "one printed
coupon per flagship", section 3.1 "owner feedback is evidence"): export
it, print it on ffc5p, read each rung three ways, mark the one that feels
right, and paste the result back into `README.md`. Every figure below
comes from the generated coupon card in this file; where the card and
the prose ever disagree, the card wins, and
`coupon/test/reference.test.ts` fails when the card and the coupon build
disagree. Nothing here has been printed or measured.

Run every command from the repo root, `hackbuild-fab`.

## Why the coupon

The switch draws its spring beam at 0.55 mm for a peak near 158 g (computed
from the build), but a 0.05 mm beam error (inferred, `click.beamPrintErrorMm`)
moves the peak from 108 g to 224 g (computed), and the PETG modulus is a
working value (tier inferred). The coupon prints the three beams side by
side, so you feel the spread on your printer and pick the beam the switch
draws. Force goes with the cube of the beam, so a small caliper difference
is a large force difference.

## Print notes

- Layer grid as the card's `layer grid:` line (profile, vendor).
- The frame, the three springs and the three cups print in PETG; the three
  caps and the spring tips print in TPU 95A. Nothing conducts.
- The rungs are marked by debossed bars past the slot end: one bar for
  the thinnest beam, three for the thickest (see the rung table).
- Print time and filament are unknown: the coupon has not been sliced by
  the harness.

## Assemble

1. Let the plate cool and peel the frame, the springs with their pink
   tips, the caps and the cups. Keep each spring by the slot whose bars
   match its rung; the springs look alike.
2. Read (a) below before a spring goes in its slot.
3. Drop each spring into its slot standing on edge, tip down. The groove
   in each end block drops over the rib on the slot floor; wiggle it until
   it seats, it should not need pushing. Upside down it will not click.
4. Drop a cap into each cap well, flange down, onto the spring stem.
5. Press each cap a few times by hand. It should click. Out of the slot a
   spring feels soft, and that is expected: an arch only snaps when its
   ends cannot move, which is what the ribs and the slot are for.

## Read each rung

Read the rungs in the order one bar, two bars, three bars. Write every
reading down as you take it; a reading you did not take stays blank.

(a) Calipers. On each spring, before it goes in, close the calipers on an
arch halfway between an end block and the centre shuttle. Take three
readings across the two arches. The drawn beams are on the card; the
slicer planned the 0.55 mm beam 0.587 to 0.601 mm wide (computed, README
Virtual slice), so a reading over the drawn beam is expected (inferred).

(b) Kitchen scale. Set the assembled coupon on a kitchen scale and zero
it, so the frame, springs and caps read as nothing. Press one cap
straight down, slowly, with a pencil eraser or a fingertip on its centre,
until it snaps. The largest number the scale shows before the snap is the
peak in grams. Three presses per rung. If the scale has a hold or peak
mode, use it; whether your scale has one is unknown here, and a scale
that updates slowly reads low (inferred).

(c) Coins, the second method or the fallback without a scale. Set a cup
on a cap, cone down, so it sits centred. Drop US five cent coins in one
at a time, gently, until the cap snaps. Count the coins in the cup when
it snaps. A tall stack stands above the cup rim and can topple; steady it
beside, never on top. The cup weighs 6.287 g solid (computed) and the cap
also rests on the spring (cap mass unknown), so the peak is about coins
times 5.000 g (vendor) plus the cup, a little over the coin count alone
(computed). Once per rung is enough; a second count if the first stack
toppled.

(d) Feel. Press each cap with the finger you would use on the switch and
give the rung one word: too soft, right or too stiff. This word decides;
(a) to (c) record why.

## Pass, and what to record

The coupon passes when every rung snaps and returns, and each rung has a
word. Anything else is a result to record, never a reprint until it
passes:

- a rung that does not snap, latches down, or does not return;
- a beam with a gap, or a beam the slicer dropped;
- a cup that tips off the cap before the snap;
- every rung too soft, or every rung too stiff.

The rule the record feeds: the rung marked right sets the switch's
default beam `spring.t` in `chase.ts` to that rung's drawn beam (the
card's rung table), since the same drawn beam on the same printer prints
the same way (inferred). Two rungs marked right: name the one you prefer.
No rung marked right: the ladder misses, and the next coupon moves past
the end you marked too soft or too stiff.

enter a coupon result or raise a profile tier"). An agent may read the
block you paste and propose the change to `chase.ts`, the press claim and
