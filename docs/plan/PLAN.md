## 1. What this is

Roughly sixty parametric design tools, sims and generators were built as single-file HTML pages and
Python generators. They share code that was retyped every time, and they share a body of corrections
that lived only in prose. hackbuild-fab consolidates both. The code becomes packages. The
corrections become executable checks.

The thesis: a physical thing is a typed document whose features declare what must be true, a
deterministic build that makes geometry from it, and a check registry that proves the declarations
against the geometry. The model writes declarations. The tool proves them.

| owner | owns |
|---|---|
| the tool | geometry, physics, collision, fits, printability, routing, nesting, export, the legal interval of every parameter, and the whole user interface |
| the model or the person | what the thing is, what it must do, and which legal option is good, written as a `chase.ts` and a `build.ts` |
| a person | looking at the render; the visual review gate in section 17 keeps this step with a person (D-154) |

The ratchet: a failure becomes a knowledge record, the record becomes a check with an origin, and
the check runs on every design sharing the kernel. A fix found in one design is ported to its
package in the same milestone, and `lineage.json` records every copy of a kernel and the fixes each
carries (D-156).

The printed conductor is the claim to lead with when the question is novelty (section 2.2). The
day-to-day work of the tool is wider: a coin with flush inlay, a print-in-place hinge, a nested
laser sheet and a vinyl copper layout are each first-class. "Not a general CAD modeller" describes
the kernel's vocabulary (section 2.5); capability beyond that vocabulary enters as imported meshes
and the opt-in BRep route.

Decides: D-154, D-156

## 2. Scope, claim and kernel

### 2.1 Scope

Mechanical, cosmetic and electrical things across FDM multi-material (conductive filament circuits
included), laser sheet, vinyl copper and PCB. Paper is a narrow fourth process: card stock
thicknesses in profile, a holder fit class, and an `impose` export (tiled inserts, crop ticks, a
print-at-100-percent note). Evidence for paper: COTTER kit.js, badge pouch cards, badge-replicator
presets (D-018).

The corpus sets the scope. Of 59 registry entries (as of 2026-10-04, Appendix B),
`registry/tools.json` classes 29 as mechanical or cosmetic, 23 as electrical, 6 as device and 1 as
meta. A KNURL counter, a SLUG vending machine, a DAVIT arm, a COLLAR coin, a LOCKET case and an OGEE
display carry no conductor, and each is first-class.

| domain | what "done well" means |
|---|---|
| mechanical | fits that land, motion proven by sweep and trial, assemblability and driver reach checked from declarations, the mechanism canon composable, printed and cut and mixed builds each with their own fit classes |
| cosmetic | art placed with real handles, flush inlay with exact boundaries, edge profiles, chamfers and rounds as declared features, multi-material that survives the slicer, printability enforced before filament burns |
| electrical | printed conductors with derived resistance, buried trace stacks that route, vinyl copper routed and gated, PCBs through two backends with independent reparse and a provenance gate, ports typed so bad compositions refuse |

Shop work stays out by default: lumber, paint, yardage, crew scheduling and costing. GUSSET stays a
HeatSync tool; its MaxRects with five sort orders and its first-fit-decreasing lumber packing are
mined into `nest` (D-019, owner).

### 2.2 The claim, narrowed

The novelty claim covers multi-material printed nets. The build derives the nets from the conductive
geometry it produced, computes each net's resistance from printed cross section, path length and a
per-direction resistivity, solves the DC operating point, and checks the voltage against the
thresholds of the pin the net lands on. For multi-material printed nets none that does this was
found in the surveyed literature. The surveyed tools take design intent in (a schematic, an
electrode list or drawn traces) and put geometry out (D-020, C-13).

Flat PCB netlist extraction exists: CAM compares Gerber copper to IPC-D-356. Altium 3D-MID holds
nets on a single STEP or IGES substrate. The claim is therefore limited to printed and cross-process
conductive regions with anisotropic resistance.

The resistance behind that wording comes from a field solve over the built conductive bodies. A
sum of path length over mean cross section gives one answer for two nets of equal length
and mean area whatever their Z transitions, pad fan-outs, T-junctions or key cones, and those are
where printed nets lose resistance: the PIP preset puts the through row at 4 times the along row
(2000 against 500 ohm cm, inferred). `circuit` voxelises each net and solves Laplace with a
per-cell anisotropic conductivity (section 9.1), so a reviewer's counter-case with the same length
and area but different via geometry gets a different number. The 1D estimate stays as a preview
and both are recorded.

Public wording follows D-020 verbatim. The words "first", "only", "nobody" and "nothing else" never
appear in public copy. The v3 sentences "KiCad knows nets and not solids. Fusion knows solids and
not nets. Nothing holds both." are retired. PRIOR-ART.md section 1 carries the claim, its one-line
form and the survey.

### 2.5 Kernel

The kernel is a restricted vocabulary: prism, stack, revolve with radial displacement fields, raster
slab, lathe, trace, unions of those, and declared chamfers, rounds, edge profiles, windows and webs
constructed at declaration time. That vocabulary built every design in the corpus. The `MeshRoute`
union names the routes: raster, prism, stack, revolve, lathe, trace, csg, imported, brep; each route
declares its guarantees (manifold, exact Z, per-slot disjoint) (D-053).

The ceiling of the vocabulary is organic and sculptural surfaces. Imported meshes (STL, GLB, STEP,
WRL) are first-class part sources, placeable and checkable with a provenance tier. BRep is an opt-in
route, `brep`, through replicad 1.1.0 (MIT) and its kernel replicad-opencascadejs 1.1.0 (48.9 MB, LGPL-2.1-only,
loaded as a separate replaceable file; vendor npm metadata), loaded lazily at M14. It never enters the default offline bake and is never a model's default. Checks read
Part IR only, so a BRep part is checked exactly like a kernel part (D-024). Three general browser
CAD attempts (WebCAD, Sketchpile, CADFORGE) were set aside, and 2026 benchmarks show models fail at
sweeps, lofts and freeform, the operations BRep adds.

### 2.6 Endgame

A person drags a proven circuit from the canon onto a face of any part and it lands: pockets cut,
conductors placed, ports exposed, checks green. That is one command, `arch.apply(part, face, id,
params)`, writing the placement into `chase.applied` and any material slot the part lacks. The
next `part.build` merges each applied entry into its part's PartDraft before kernel stage 1, so the
archetype's bodies and features become ordinary Part IR (D-165). The command ships at M06; the mouse gesture on the same command ships at M15. The canon package is
`archetypes` (was `circuits`) beside `mechanisms`, with commands `arch.find`, `arch.apply`,
`mech.find`, `mech.derive` (D-014).

## 5. Architecture

Packages sit in tiers and import downward only. The table lives in `tools/tiers.json`, and the CI
`tiers` job fails on any import edge the table does not list (D-044).

| tier | packages | may import |
|---|---|---|
| S | spec | nothing at runtime |
| 0 | profile, catalog, geom, mesh | S and lower tier 0 entries listed in tiers.json |
| 1 | 3mf, sim, circuit, copper, board, nest, inlay, motion, wiring, constraints, mechanisms, archetypes | S, 0, and tier 1 entries listed in tiers.json |
| 2 | checks, project, reference, knowledge, chase | S, 0, 1 |
| 3 | ui-core, ui, link | S, 0, 1, 2 |
| app | core (hbf), apps/fab, apps/sketch, apps/blocks | everything |

Three moves from the scaffold: `catalog` drops to tier 0 so `board` stops importing upward; `chase`
holds the schema and validator only, and the renderer binding moves to `ui-core`; kernels and canons
register their `ConstraintDef` entries into `constraints` at load, so the solver depends on no
kernel (inversion, no cycle).

Workspace packages are import boundaries and are never released on their own. Every one is `private: true`, all
version together, and only `@hackbuild/kernel` publishes (D-036), so a package split costs one row
in `tools/tiers.json` and no release work. The splits that look fine-grained carry weight:
`3mf`, `inlay` and `copper` leave by `git subtree split` at their triggers (D-027, D-028, D-029),
which needs each in its own directory, and `constraints` must sit below every kernel that
registers into it.

Tiers S to 2 import no `document`, `window` or `vue`; a lint enforces it. The same kernel code runs
in node sweeps and in the browser. KNUCKLE's canvas-dependent kernel gets a headless port before it
becomes a reference (D-045).

`@hackbuild/spec` is a private workspace package split one contract per file: ids, schema, part-ir,
features, board-features, feature-kinds, build, checks, constraints, profile, circuit, catalog,
canon, sim, registry, chase, hook, commands, errors, capabilities, index. It has zero runtime
dependencies and passes `tsc --strict` with `noUncheckedIndexedAccess` and
`exactOptionalPropertyTypes`. zod 4 schemas live in packages and are asserted with `satisfies`
against the spec types; JSON Schema is derived from them (D-046). One `KernelId` union is used by
`CheckDef.needs`, `Bound.kernel`, `ChaseMeta.kernels` and `tools.json`; it includes `3mf`, `board`
and `inlay`, which 16 seeded checks already use (computed, `jq` over the seed, D-057).

### 5.1 Data flow

```
chase.ts + build.ts
   | params in, declarations out
PartDraft / AssemblyDraft
   | kernel stages 1 to 8 (section 6.4)
Part IR  (bodies, solid, features, frame, metrics, warnings, buildHash)
   | registry checks read Part IR only
CheckResult[]  ->  CommandResult  (evidence, never prose)
   | export.run behind the gate (section 14.3)
artifacts (3MF, STL, DXF, SVG, gerbers, KiCad, Circuit JSON)
   | every object stored by content hash
<workspace>/.hbf/store  addressed as hbf://
```

ARCHITECTURE.md states what each arrow carries, the runtime contexts (node CLI, stateless MCP
server, browser app, `file://` bake) and the extension points.

Nine routes form the `MeshRoute` union (D-053) because a route is what an author declares and what
checks read guarantees from. In `mesh` three of them lower to another route and share its code:

| route | lowers to | what it adds before lowering |
|---|---|---|
| stack | prism, one per band | band overlap of 0.01 to 0.02 mm enforced |
| lathe | revolve with a heightfield field | texture heightfield sampling (TOGGLE) |
| trace | prism | raster trace, DP simplify to a stated tolerance, ear clip (SPRUE) |

`MeshRouteDef.lowersTo` in `spec/build.ts` records this, so a fix to prism or revolve reaches the
routes above it in one place.

### 5.2 Addressing and hashes

`hbf://` URIs point into `<workspace>/.hbf/store/` keyed by content hash for builds, meshes, reports
and artifacts, plus static resources for checks, feature kinds, canon entries, references, profile
rows and knowledge records. MCP 2026-07-28 removed sessions, so the MCP server holds no state; the
store is the state (D-050).

| hash | definition | decision |
|---|---|---|
| canonical JSON | RFC 8785 over every hashed input | D-051 |
| meshHash | int32 grid coordinates `round(v * 1024)` after the 1/1024 snap, plus triangles rotated to their smallest index with winding kept, then sorted; an inside-out twin hashes different (AGENT-SURFACE.md 7.3) | D-051, D-175 |
| BuildInputs | defHash, paramsHash, profileHash, registryHash, kernelVersion, engines (manifold, rapier, ngspice), adapter, seed, sourceDateEpoch, licences | D-051 |
| build hash | hash of canonical BuildInputs; two builds with one hash are the same build | D-051 |
| registryHash | sha256 over sorted (kind, id, version) of every registry entry the build resolved | D-051 |
| artifactHash | sha256 over sorted (entry name, sha256 of uncompressed bytes) with date metadata normalised | D-052 |

Each `CheckDef` keeps its own semver because `CheckResult.version` (D-064) is read outside the repo:
in exported reports, reference goldens and check origins. A major bump means a verdict can flip on
the same Part IR. The golden reports under `reference/<area>/<id>/expected/` catch a change, and the
CI `parity` job fails when a golden `report.json` changes while the check's version did not, so a
version cannot fall behind its behaviour. Package manifests are generated from the registrations at
build time, and `Manifest.version` equals the `@hackbuild/kernel` version (D-010, D-036).
`registryHash` stays a hash of resolved entry versions rather than a git tree hash: the `file://`
bake has no git, and a tree hash would move every build hash, and with it every export approval
(D-109) and loop key (D-111), on a comment edit.

Byte-identical 3MF needs fixed dates, fixed mtimes, no directory entries and canonical deflate
(C-08). Each writer reproduces its own bytes under `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH`: fixed CreationDate and
ModificationDate, fixed UTC zip mtimes, no directory entries, sorted entries, a fixed deflate level.
The js and py writers must agree on entry contents and artifactHash; byte identity across the two
writers is outside the requirement. Evidence: one input gave py 3979 B and js 4243 B (measured);
JSZip adds `3D/`, `Metadata/` and `_rels/` entries; two py runs differ at byte 11; the date stamps
sit at `orca3mf.js` lines 91 and 247 and `orca3mf.py` lines 193 and 384 (D-052).

## 6. Part IR and feature kinds

A part is a set of bodies, their union as `solid`, a typed feature list, a frame declaration,
metrics, warnings and a build hash. `spec/part-ir.ts` is the contract.

### 6.1 Bodies, metrics, frames

`Part.bodies` is `{id, mesh, material, slot, role}[]` with `solid` as their union; a feature may
name its `body`. COLLAR inlay, PIP keys, the KNUCKLE flip group and TENON parts are multi-body
(D-048).

FITS rows, spring curves, drive readouts, routing warnings and clamps with their reasons are
`metrics` and `warnings` in the IR and hash with the build, so a FITS row can never lag its geometry
(D-049).

Frame rules are enforced at emit: origin on the joint axis, `carriesOffset` declared, poses
immutable, print pose a reorientation with volume parity. Sub parts printed in another orientation
declare `pose` with volume parity asserted. Face-down art and keyed features on flipped parts carry
a `mirrorOnFlip` assertion; every multicolour case in the corpus mirrored by hand. Cut-sheet sensor
stacks declare `stack` (layer role, material, outline registration, windows) so KEYWAY gates run on
any surface (D-063).

### 6.2 Kinds, the contract and admission

A feature is geometry on one part that a generator makes and a check proves. Every feature kind is
a generator, a non-empty check set and a fit class, with a reference. `FeatureKindDef` requires
`generate`, non-empty `checks`, `fit` (a function or `"none"`), non-empty `reference`, `schema` and
`version`, so a kind missing any part fails to type-check (D-058). Names are camelCase; the loader
accepts `tabslot` and `flexcut` as aliases for one minor version (D-017).

22 kinds carry over from v3: bore, pocket, boss, chamfer, round, edgeProfile, window, web, tabSlot,
snap, screw, hinge, conductor, contact, port, pad, horn, detent, finger, flexCut, lamination,
bridge. Their fields carry over from v3 section 4 with camelCase names; `conductor` gains width and
t, `contact` gains gap and coneH, `port` gains a read model (section 9.4), and `pocket` gains the
`sealed` variant (section 6.3).

Four v3 kinds sat at another level of abstraction and leave the union (D-177,
`MOVED_FEATURE_KINDS` in spec/features.ts):

| v3 kind | now | where |
|---|---|---|
| latch | mechanism `displacement-coincidence-latch`; `apply()` emits features, a gated metric and an `identity` assertion CHK-MOT-05 proves | spec/canon.ts, `Author.mech` in spec/build.ts |
| coupon | a reference under `reference/coupons/<id>/` declaring `Chase.coupon` | spec/chase.ts `CouponDecl` |
| pivot | `JointDecl` revolute with `via` and bought `stock` | spec/part-ir.ts |
| pinInSlot | `JointDecl` prismatic or planar with `via` and `range`, plus a `contact` assertion over range | spec/part-ir.ts |

A kind is usable in a build only from the milestone whose def compiles with a generator, a check and
a reference. `FEATURE_KIND_ADMISSION` names that milestone per kind; `FeatureAt<M>` is what build.ts
may declare at M, and `FeatureKindRegistryAt<M>` makes the kernel's registry fail tsc if an
admitted kind lacks a complete def (D-177). M00 admits no kind. The 2026-10-04 gaps (hinge, finger,
lamination and bridge lack a check; boss, chamfer, round and snap lack a reference; round has no
generator in harvested code) hold those kinds back to their milestone:

| milestone | kinds admitted |
|---|---|
| M03 | pocket, inlay |
| M05 | conductor, contact, port, pad |
| M06 | bore, window, detent, finger, flexCut, spring |
| M10 | hinge, thread, bayonet, magnet, insert, relief |
| M11 | boss, tabSlot, snap, chamfer, round, edgeProfile, web, screw, horn, lamination, bridge, mount |

The `detent` kind keeps its name; the DETENT machine profile id becomes `detent-28byj` (D-016,
owner). `detent` and `spring` hold strip and beam geometry; their governing numbers come from the
mechanisms `count-wheel-detent` and `click-spring-key` as gated metrics (D-177).

### 6.4 Generator stage order

`build.ts` returns a `PartDraft` or `AssemblyDraft` of declarations. The kernel runs, per body then
per part (D-047):

1 base shape through its route; 2 pre-union cutters (chamfer, round); 3 union positives; 4
post-union cutters; 5 partition by role priority (flex-conductor, flex, conductor, art, body); 6
insets (coincidence and pinch, the CHK-3MF-04 parameters); 7 frame emit; 8 hash.

Chamfer after union is unexpressible in this order. Part sources are `kernel`, `imported` (with a
provenance tier) and `brep` (opt-in, lazy). Bought bodies (servos, boards, displays) are first-class
in an assembly with body and flange envelopes.

## 8. Constraints and checks

### 8.1 The interval solver

Ask what a dimension can be and `constraints` returns a bounded range, each bound with the kernel
that set it, a reason and a source. Kernels and canons register `ConstraintDef` entries at load
(D-044). A query reports which bound binds. When floors exceed ceilings the result is `E_UNSAT` with
`legal: null` and the smallest change that would fit. This is what turns generate-and-test into
generate-from-legal.

A resistance budget sets a width floor. From R = rho L / (w t), a maximum resistance R_budget gives
the floor w = rho L / (R_budget t). v3 printed the budget as a 1.20 ceiling and the band 0.80 to
1.20 was empty at its own numbers (PLAN v3 lines 319 to 326, C-01). The corrected query (D-072):

```
hbf constraints.query conductor.width --chase keys-flex-pair \
    --feature conductor.key1 \
    --facts material=cpla,flexes=true,netBudgetOhm=8000
  legal 3.23 to 6.00 mm      binding: floor 3.23, resistance
  floor 0.80  profile  printability, line width 0.40, two perimeters  CHK-PRT-04
  floor 0.80  profile  fatigue, cpla across layer lines under flex    knowledge:04-heuristics
  floor 3.23  circuit  8000 ohm budget, 62.0 mm path, 0.72 thick,
                       30 ohm cm (vendor, Protopasta printed XY)     CHK-NET-05
  ceil  6.00  mesh     key pitch 8.0 minus 2 x 1.0 clearance
```

| case | rho | path | t | budget | width floor | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protopasta printed XY (vendor) | 30 ohm cm | 62 mm | 0.72 mm | 8 kohm | 3.23 mm (computed: 300 x 62 / (8000 x 0.72)) | legal 3.23 to 6.00 |
| PIP preset cPLA along (inferred) | 500 ohm cm | 62 mm | 0.72 mm | 8 kohm | 53.8 mm (computed: 53.82) | `E_UNSAT`; the budget that fits the 6.00 ceiling is 71.8 kohm (computed: 71759.26 ohm, printed 71760, floors round up) |
| Protopasta printed XY (vendor) | 30 ohm cm | 62 mm | 0.72 mm | 2 kohm | 12.92 mm (computed) | `E_UNSAT`; fits 6.00 at 4306 ohm (computed) |

Every number in a message is formatted from the computation that produced the bound, and a golden
test compares message numbers against recomputation (M01). AGENT-SURFACE.md section 6 shows the full

### 8.4 The error contract

`CheckResult` is `{id, version, verdict, severity, target, measured, legal, constraint, origin,
fix?, extra?}`, with `origin` an `hbf://check/<id>` URI. Interval `LegalValues` carry `nearest`. A
`Fix` is `{param (JSON pointer), set, predicts}`. `CheckDef.drivenBy` names the params that move a
check. A missing or NaN measured value is `E_PARAM_MISSING` (D-064).

Codes: E_SCHEMA, E_REF (with `didYouMean`), E_UNSAT, E_HANDLE, E_STALE, E_PROVENANCE, E_GATE,
E_GATE_UNAVAILABLE, E_REPEAT, E_KERNEL, E_PARAM_MISSING; AGENT-SURFACE.md defines each.

The message template is `<what failed> <measured> <unit>, legal <set>, set by <constraint>`, then a
fix line. `CheckTarget` covers part, body, feature, assembly, sheet, net, artifact, source, page and
project; 19 project-level seed checks could not run under the old `CheckDef.run` (computed over
hbf/seed/checks.seed.json); after the retarget they are 7 artifact, 7 source, 2 page and 3 project
rows (CHECKS.md section 4, D-065).

Severity has three values, the ones the seeds already use: `block` stops fabrication export, `warn`
is shown, `note` is informational (D-067). Verdict (`pass`, `warn`, `fail`, `skip`) says what
happened on one target; severity says what a fail of that check stops. No severity stops the build.
Every build completes and stores its Part IR, so a failing finding can be rendered, probed and
located, and a fix can be predicted by a re-run. A kernel that cannot produce geometry returns
`E_KERNEL`, a command error and never a check result. The seeds carry 76 block and 9 warn rows
(Appendix B), with no mapping step between seed and registry.

## 9. Electrical

### 9.1 Pipeline

`circuit` extracts nets from conductive bodies, computes each net's bulk resistance with a voxel
conductance solve over those bodies, and solves the DC operating point analytically in the offline
bake. eecircuit-engine 1.8.0 (MIT wrapper; the bundled ngspice 45.2 wasm is BSD-3-Clause AND
LGPL-2.0-or-later for numparam and KLU, so it loads as a lazy separate file and never enters a bake,
D-043; one 19 MB ESM file in a
43 MB package, vendor npm) loads lazily for device models and transients. Timings are one machine's
measurement: start 449 ms, `.op` median 0.365 ms (measured on this host, verified-claims C9)
(D-086). `circuit` answers "does it work"; `board` answers "can it be fabbed".

Bulk resistance (`VoxelSolve`, `NetResistance` in `spec/circuit.ts`):

| step | rule |
|---|---|
| grid | cells of half the line width in X and Y and one layer height in Z: 0.2, 0.2, 0.24 mm on ffc5p with the 0.4 preset; a cell is conductive when its centre is inside the body |
| size | a 62 mm by 1.4 mm by 0.72 mm trace is 6,510 cells and a 12 mm PIP touch pad 0.72 mm thick about 8,480 (computed), so a net solves in a dependency-free loop |
| conductivity | a diagonal tensor per cell from the along, across and through rows (9.6); through is +Z; along is the declared raster angle when the conductor declares one, else the side-wall tangent for cells within the perimeters, else an in-plane geometric mean of along and across for interior cells (inferred rule until a coupon or a declaration replaces it) |
| solve | finite volume, two-point flux with each cell's conductivity projected on the face normal; terminal cells held at 1 V and 0 V; conjugate gradient with a Jacobi preconditioner in plain TypeScript, relative residual 1e-8; a solve that hits the iteration cap marks the net unknown |
| contacts | material interfaces join perfectly inside the solve; contact rows add in series afterwards, so none counts twice |
| error | two-point flux is exact for grid-aligned frames; references solve straight traces at 0 and 45 degrees and a half-cell refinement, and a change above 2 percent raises a build warning |
| preview | the 1D path estimate (length over mean area per direction) runs while dragging; checks and exports require the solve; both values and their ratio land in Part IR metrics (D-049) |

A net carries three electrical quantities, each typed in `spec/circuit.ts`:

| quantity | where it comes from | qualifier until a coupon |
|---|---|---|
| bulk resistance | voxel solve over the conductive bodies with per-cell anisotropic conductivity from the resistivity rows (9.6); 1D path estimate as preview | inferred (PIP preset) |
| contact resistance | one series element per terminal and per cPLA to cTPU interface, priced from a profile `ContactRow` (`spec/profile.ts`) | unknown: no row exists, so the total is a lower bound and the net lists its unpriced contacts |
| capacitance to ground and to neighbour nets | parallel plate over the buried stack (9.5) with constant `petg.epsR`; while that constant has no source, PIP's rule of pin capacitance plus 0.02 pF per mm plus 1 pF (inferred, pip-controller `src/model.js:418`) and no coupling | inferred |

`McuPinSpec` carries input capacitance and leakage with their own source: PIP models 7 pF and 1 nA
(inferred, `src/layout.js:46`), and no datasheet figure is recorded for RP2040 or ESP32-C3.

## 13. CHASE (working name)

A chase is a typed document that fully describes a design: meta, materials, params, presets, build
reference, expect, waive, exports, assertions, applied, stack, motion. Its bench layout (shell,
views, rail, welcome, status, slots) lives beside it in `bench.ts`, built with `defineBench`, which
refuses a rail panel naming no param group and a welcome preset naming no preset. `defHash` covers
the chase and `build.ts` only, so a layout edit moves no build hash, export approval or `E_REPEAT`
key; `chase.scaffold` writes a default `bench.ts` from the params, so a model never needs to touch
it (D-174, `spec/bench.ts`). Hand both to the component set and a working bench results. Nothing in
either is HTML or CSS.

A chase is a TypeScript module written through `defineChase` and type-checked against
`@hackbuild/spec`; a zod schema is derived from it. `meta.chase` is the schema version, 2, with
migrations (`migrate/1-to-2.ts`) and a deep normalise on load, so no stored document yields NaN
geometry; badge-replicator's shallow merges produced NaN (D-131).

No command executes a chase. Its source is one `export const chase = defineChase({...})` over a
literal object, which `packages/chase` parses without importing or evaluating it, so
`chase.validate`, `chase.diff`, `chase.patch` and the app read data; `chase.patch` edits byte spans
of single literals and reparses before writing. `build.ts` is the only code, and it runs in a child
node process with read access to its design directory and the kernel, no writes, an import
allowlist, network globals removed and a 30 s wall budget (inferred plan budget). A bake embeds the
chase as JSON and `build.ts` as bundled JavaScript (D-178; AGENT-SURFACE.md 3.7, ARCHITECTURE.md
11.2).

The rule that keeps it from becoming a language: a chase declares and never computes. No
expressions, conditionals or loops. Conditionals live in `build.ts`. A derived param names a
function. The 2026 benchmarks support this: Text2CAD-Bench gives DeepSeek-V3.2 L1 Geo invalidity of
13.3 percent on CadQuery code (Table 1) against 67.3 percent on command sequences (Table 3)
(measured in the paper, arXiv 2605.18430).

Revised fields (D-132): `ChaseMeta` gains `machines[]` (was `machine`), `license` (SPDX), `derivedFrom {id, buildHash}` and `aliases`; `Chase` gains `presets`,
`applied` (written by `arch.apply`), `expect` (declared violation sets), `waive` (D-173), and loses `checks`; `shell` (paper or instrument), `status` and the
rail moved to the bench (D-174), where `rail` is optional because the `surface` kind has none; `ParamField` gains `label`, `help` (one line,
replacing rail paragraphs), `constraintDim` (the solver's live interval becomes the slider range),
`commit` (touch, commit, light) and `step` on numeric types; `ExportDecl.kind` is an exporter
registry id, and gating comes from `ExporterDef.fabrication`, so png and json are free while 3mf,
cut and gerbers are gated everywhere.

Six kinds are fixed: `single`, `composer`, `surface`, `board`, `machine`, `catalog`. A seventh needs
a measured layout sharing nothing with the six. Bench slots name a component for a region and are the
valve that keeps the format from growing a feature for every one-off (D-126).

`chase.validate` returns a `CommandResult` with `CommandError[]` and typed questions from the
completeness audit, never strings (D-133). Writes go to a branch `hbf/<chase>` through
`chase.patch`, whose anchors must match exactly once (`E_STALE` otherwise) or whose JSON pointers
must resolve (D-115).

The loop: write a chase and a build function, validate, check, render, look. The surface where a
model can be wrong is the geometry and the schema; the UI, the exports and the bake are written
once. A site section is a `catalog` chase whose items are other chases.

Decides: D-115, D-126, D-131, D-132, D-133, D-174, D-178

protocol error.

### 14.3 The export gate

Fabrication exports run build and checks first and never ask for a failing build. With elicitation,
the server returns `input_required` with one flat form and an HMAC-SHA256 `requestState` binding
command, args digest, build hash, a 10-minute expiry and a single-use nonce recorded in
`.hbf/gate-used.jsonl`; a moved build hash re-asks. Without elicitation the result is
`E_GATE_UNAVAILABLE` naming the CLI line. The host asks on `mcp__hbf__export_run`. The CLI needs a
TTY and has no bypass flag. The prompt says approval does not identify who approved. No standing
environment token exists (D-109).

## 17. Testing

Ten levels (D-152): 1 unit (Vitest 5, every push); 2 property (fast-check 4.10, 100 runs on push,
2000 nightly); 3 contract (registry conformance); 4 parity triple (geometry matches, params
round-trip, one edit gives the expected delta); 5 golden (build, report, artifact hashes and message
numbers, never overwritten in CI); 6 design harness (the five tests); 7 3MF corpus (`validate_3mf.py
--export` and `--roundtrip`); 8 browser (Playwright 1.63: BenchHook conformance, smoke, overflow at
390, 768, 1280); 9 visual review (a person, every PR changing a meshHash); 10 agent eval (Inspect,
weekly and before release).

The five tests per design are the bar a design reaches before it is done: cases (fixed named
designs), sweep (fast-check with a seed, path replay and shrinking), liveness (every param moves the
meshHash or a metric), smoke (through BenchHook at 390, 768 and 1280 px, console errors, pink
budget, touch-only pass) and islands (the exported 3MF sliced for unsupported regions). Only PIP had
all five; KNUCKLE, TENON and COTTER shipped subsets (D-152).

Level 2 carries a fuzz job from M00: fast-check over the 3MF reader (traversal names, entry counts,
inflation bombs) and the share decoder (oversize, unversioned and malformed payloads), each case
refused with E_SCHEMA and the rule named (D-166).

Harnesses assert and exit non-zero. A harness that did not run is reported as not run. PIP's
harnesses print and exit 0 behind a `FAIL|THROW` grep, which is the pattern this replaces (D-153).

The determinism job builds every reference on ubuntu x64 and arm64 with equal mesh hashes (D-056).
The 3MF corpus holds the gold round trip, the 25 owner 3MFs, legacy bench exports and known-bad
fixtures (CASK empty plate, TENON undefined names, a numeric `overhang_fan_threshold`). Goldens live
under `reference/<area>/<id>/expected/` and `hbf parity --accept` is the only updater of the JSON
goldens; section renders have one named writer each (D-227).

PRs that change any meshHash get before and after section, assembled and plate PNGs and a required
checkbox "I looked at the renders"; a person looks at desktop and 390 px. The OGEE crank passed
every numeric check and read as loose sticks (D-154). Agent evals are Inspect tasks from worked
sessions, references, one-param variants and seeded failures, scored by build hash and verdict
counts with no model judge, per model tier, with a held-out split (D-155).

TESTING.md holds the CI job table, cross-implementation agreement, the nightly slicer round trip and
firmware compile rules.

Decides: D-152, D-153, D-155, D-227

## 21. Milestones

Every milestone starts by wiring its adopted dependency and proving parity against it before own
code. Every feature kind, check, archetype, mechanism or export admitted in a milestone gets its
reference in that milestone. A milestone is green only with references passing conformance and the
house-rules, derived and tiers CI jobs green. Pins are re-dated at each exit (D-157). The agent
surface grows from M00 (D-114).

lets M00 start; exit B (the print and read) freezes the spec parts the path ran. M04 proceeds on
the virtual exit B with a provisional dependency on the print (refined by D-234).

## 24. What this is for

Near term: the benches re-hosted as chases on shared kernels, plus FAB, SKETCH, BLOCKS, the
reference library and hbf. A working day afterwards: describe a thing, get a chase and a build
function back, watch the checks run, look at the render, adjust the geometry, bake it. The UI, the
exports, the layout and the offline file are written once and left alone. A button is `arch.apply`
of one of four proven designs. A mechanism is a composition from the canon. A new material is a
coupon and a row in profile, entered by a person.

The second-order effect compounds. Every check prunes the search space for every later design. The
journal check that rejects a square peg rejects it in every future mechanism. A model working inside
a heavily constrained space explores a smaller space with a better shape, so it reaches a good
design in fewer tries. The constraint machinery is what makes the interesting work cheap.

Extraction follows the claim: hackbuild-3mf at M00 exit, then hackbuild-inlay when COLLAR and SPRUE
build on it, then hackbuild-copper when BLOCKS and KEYWAY build on it. Profile and circuit stay
internal and get built early, since resistivity and process loss are what the claim rests on. Each
fix found along the way returns to its package in the same milestone, recorded in `lineage.json`.
