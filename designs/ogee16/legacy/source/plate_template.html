parts

Click a row to drop that part off the plates. Quantities are for one complete machine.

plate

256 cube, drawn to scale with the height cage. ONE PART TYPE PER PLATE: more plates, but each is a single print profile with one set of settings and one failure mode, and you can reprint a whole family without disturbing anything else. Everything is inset 10mm from the bed edge (dashed). Parts may turn 90 degrees but none is tipped: every orientation was picked by scoring all 24 axis-aligned poses for bed contact, overhang and height.

export

One STL per plate with every part already positioned, so it drops into Orca at 0,0 with nothing to arrange. Or a single part alone.

by hand

No servos. Each bar keeps the crank the servo used to turn; crank and shaft print as one piece, the shaft passes out through a bore in the wall, and a knurled knob presses onto the D at the end. Turn a row knob and a column knob together and that pixel latches. Turn either alone and the rod lifts 4.249mm, short of the 7.6 it needs, and drops back. The latch plate has a finger tab out through the -X wall: push it 3mm and everything drops.

print notes

Bars print lugs-up with no supports. The frame prints as separate flat plates and tabs together exactly as the cut version does; every profile is shrunk 0.075 all round, which thins each tab and widens each slot in one move. Running clearances are 0.30 rather than the laser version's 0.40. Pivot pins go in head-down; the printed head replaces the cemented washer.
